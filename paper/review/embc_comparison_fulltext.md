## What this sample supports for Exchange-ML

The accepted examples contain several contribution types: a concrete QC intervention, an inspectable software tool, and a reusable dataset/evaluation resource. Universal superiority, a new large architecture, multiple clinical centers or a clinical reader-time trial are not common requirements demonstrated by these three examples. This is an inference about published examples, not a rule used by reviewers or evidence that those features are unnecessary for every submission.

For Exchange-ML the defensible target is a short, clearly bounded biomedical-imaging contribution: explain presence/partition behavior, report strong controls, use held-out evidence for its original question, and disclose endpoint dependence and failed fusion. The small optimized AI2Seg gains do not make an uncertain Exchange-ML gain statistically established. The existing PENGWIN study has different strengths (case-cluster intervals, fixed controls, mechanism perturbations) and different weaknesses (no reliable advantage over P1, shared omissions, limited external evidence while E6 is running). A clear negative or mixed result can be scientifically useful when it diagnoses a previously plausible assumption; it must be written as such.

The artifact paper also illustrates why Exchange-ML must not infer utility from a favorable metric alone: incomplete annotations change FP interpretation. The yeast paper explicitly separates semantic overlap from individual-object quality, supporting separate structural/detection/volume endpoints. AI2Seg compares annotation with model predictions; its deployment setting requires an annotation under inspection and is different from estimating unseen-case prediction quality without GT.

## Older supporting example, not counted in the recent-three quota

**McBee et al., EMBC2022**, *Image-Level Uncertainty in Pseudo-Label Selection for Semi-Supervised Segmentation*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC48229.2022.9871359), [full PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10445335/). Full HTML methods/tables/discussion were downloaded and read. Three histopathology datasets, eight initializations and20 MC-dropout samples examine a plausible uncertainty-selection assumption. MoNuSeg no-pseudo-label Dice=.72±.04 versus lowest-entropy-quartile=.66±.07; TableII uses Welch tests and discloses detrimental/neutral selection. §II distinguishes biopsies, crops and patients; exact patient-disjoint holdout sizes are not given. This is a particularly relevant example of a published analysis whose contribution includes exposing unreliable uncertainty behavior rather than universally improving a detector.
