## Results

### Evaluation setting and evidence roles

The study separates validation diagnosis, controlled mechanism checks and held-out evaluation. E1 used 40 validation CT cases, comprising 20 pelvic and 20 femur scans and yielding 80 annotated bone units. E2 applied controlled interventions to 40 bones from 20 validation cases. E3 evaluated 100 separate held-out cases, equally divided between scan types, yielding 200 bone units. Each pelvic case contributed three annotated bones, whereas each femur case contributed one. The primary pooled analysis therefore weighted bones equally, with 150 pelvic and 50 femur units in the held-out cohort. Three ensemble members and a separate Stage 2 model produced 400 held-out inference records. Validation and held-out data were never pooled to increase the sample size of a comparison. [Sources: E1/E2/E3 manifest summaries.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

Scores were computed from predictions and scan-type metadata before ground-truth risk evaluation. The foreground disagreement term `A` measures pairwise Jaccard disagreement; the combined score adds a weighted partition term `wQ`, where `w` is the shared-to-union foreground fraction. On shared foreground, the two partition normalizations are `Q1=VI/log`N`` and `Q2=VI/H`, where `N` is the shared voxel count and `H` is joint entropy. The implementation returns a zero partition term when there is no shared foreground; an empty/nonempty pair instead receives a combined score of one through its foreground term. Pairwise disagreement scores average three member pairs; entropy summarizes the ensemble mean probability map, and count variance is computed across members. Primary risk is the mean of the three members' separately evaluated normalized merge+split risks. AURC uses tie-expected prefix risk and trapezoidal integration over coverage `1/n` through 1. Paired bootstrap intervals resample cases within scan type and retain all bones and repeated sampled cases. Stage 2 provides a second risk source within the same backend family, rather than an external architecture test. [Sources: E1/E3 scoring and held-out configuration.](../../configs/stage3_h1_v2.json)

### Component diagnosis and controlled responses

Partition disagreement was present in the validation cohort, although its contribution depended strongly on normalization. Across the 80 current per-bone units, the median partition share was 6.96% in FA-v1 and 58.87% in FA-v2. Median weighted partition contributions were 0.001164 and 0.032116, respectively. Mean-pair raw VI was at most 1e−12 in 27 units (33.75%), but reached 0.601793 nats elsewhere. Thus complete agreement on partitions was common without explaining all cases. These per-bone results should not be substituted for the approximately 2% median contribution reported in the earlier whole-case analysis, which used a different scope and evaluation convention. [Source: E1_decomposition.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

Controlled interventions supported component separation while exposing limits of severity interpretation. All 32 phantom assertions passed. Among 452 eligible responses, identity and ID permutation preserved zero foreground and partition disagreement; eligible merge/split interventions produced positive partition disagreement with unchanged foreground; and the specified missing/boundary interventions affected foreground disagreement while leaving VI and `Q2` zero. The 75 ineligible response rows were retained in the accounting. However, all 11 single-fragment bones reached `Q2=1` at each of the three split levels. The normalized score therefore detected these changes without distinguishing their split multiplicities. Merge severity summaries also required matched cohorts: among the same four bones eligible at all three levels, median `Q2` increased from 0.005722 to 0.017858 to 0.032355. Medians computed over the changing cohorts of 29, 12 and four eligible bones do not establish a within-bone trend. [Sources: E2 summary, response summaries and matched-cohort extraction.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

Figure 1. [Controlled GT merge responses in matched cohorts](figures/e2_matched_merge.pdf). Panel A retains the same 12 bones at levels 1–2; Panel B retains the same four bones at levels 1–3. Thin lines show individual trajectories and the blue line the median, on a common `Q2` axis. These are descriptive intervention responses without confidence intervals or a clinical-severity interpretation. The original experiment artifacts are unchanged.

Exact count replication isolated the denominator effect in `VI/log(N)`. All 240 validation pair contingency tables were reconstructed. Multiplying every union count, including foreground-mismatch counts, by factors 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 preserved VI, joint entropy, foreground disagreement, overlap weight and `Q2`, while `Q1` followed `VI/log(rN)`. Recorded invariant and formula residuals were zero. This verifies a count-scaling mechanism, not invariance of model predictions under CT resampling. [Source: E2_replication_summary.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

### Held-out structural-risk ranking

FA-v2 met the pooled held-out criterion against both designated baselines. Its member-mean merge+split AURC was 0.3311, compared with 0.4942 for presence and 0.6358 for mean entropy. The paired differences were −0.16313 (95% CI −0.21184 to −0.11682) and −0.30470 (−0.37159 to −0.23683), respectively. Both met the configured effect floor of −0.02 with intervals below zero. The corresponding Stage 2 differences were −0.12760 (−0.18586 to −0.07138) and −0.28142 (−0.35085 to −0.21028). Femur-only member-mean intervals crossed zero for both baseline comparisons, so the pooled result does not independently establish the same benefit within each scan type. Partition-v1 and partition-v2 had lower held-out merge+split AURCs than FA-v2; FA-v2 minus these components was +0.05914 (95% CI 0.03921 to 0.08032) and +0.03333 (0.01535 to 0.05265). ### Evaluation conditions

The controlled case-level analysis supported a normalization effect within annotated scope, without establishing that scope restriction alone improved ranking. With the same case-level risk held fixed, replacing log-count normalization by joint entropy in annotated scope reduced pooled held-out AURC by 0.07030 for member-mean risk (95% CI −0.13079 to −0.01190) and by 0.06979 for Stage 2 (−0.13052 to −0.01147). Pooled whole-scope normalization differences had intervals crossing zero. Restricting scope while holding either normalization fixed also had intervals crossing zero for both risk sources. These case-level AURCs should not be compared directly with Table 1, because both the unit weighting and risk aggregation differ from the per-bone analysis. [Source: E4_case_contrasts.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

Disagreement attribution and error prevalence further constrained interpretation. On held-out femur cases, the mean across cases of the mean three pairwise outside-scope ratios was 86.55%, compared with 29.66% on pelvic cases. This measures voxels assigned by predictions to anatomy outside the declared annotation scope; it does not establish their anatomical identity from ground truth. Any-merge/split prevalence was 69% over pooled member-mean bone risks, giving a maximum binary enrichment of 1.4493, and reached 96% in femur units, where that ceiling was only 1.0417. FA-v2's pooled binary enrichment was 1.3043 and 1.3406 at the top 10% and 20% of scores, but its top-severity-quartile enrichment was only 0.5829 and 0.5371, below the random reference of one. Binary-error enrichment therefore did not demonstrate prioritization of the most severe errors. [Sources: annotation_scope_case_summary and E4_enrichment.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

The internal matching-based F1 endpoint could conceal substantial errors. Perfect F1 with more than 10% missing ground-truth volume occurred in nine validation model-bone records and seven held-out records, representing four distinct cases in each split. In held-out case 302, the third member's femur prediction had eight ground-truth and six predicted instances, two merge errors, eight split errors and 43.56% missing volume, while internal F1 remained one. Ground-truth best-IoU assignments in this evaluator are not constrained to be one-to-one. These observations concern the internal evaluator used here and should not be attributed to the official PENGWIN evaluation. [Sources: corrected E4_blindspots and E4_blindspot_counts.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

### Fixed-budget review and omission failures

The global AURC improvement did not establish benefit at the two review budgets. For pooled held-out member-mean merge+split risk, retaining 50% of bones gave risks of 0.3780 with FA-v2 and 0.5774 with presence; at 80% retention the values were 0.6041 and 0.6044. Thus improvements concentrated away from the high-coverage operating point can coexist with a small fixed-budget difference. Supplementary case-bootstrap analysis, added after inspecting held-out results, produced the differences in Table 2. The merge+split and 1−F1 intervals crossed zero at both budgets, whereas missing-volume differences favored presence. These marginal intervals were not adjusted for the family of supplementary comparisons. [Sources: E5_curve_points and E5_paired_budget.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

Review cost and complete omissions remained additional limitations. At the 40-of-200-bone budget, expected distinct CT counts were 34 for FA-v2, 32 for presence, 37 for partition-v1 and 33 for partition-v2; these are not equal-CT-cost policies or measured reading times. The rule-selected example 187/left_hip had zero foreground/partition disagreement while all three members completely missed the annotated bone, producing F1 zero and missing volume one. Its merge+split risk was zero, illustrating why that endpoint alone does not describe omission safety. The originally selected examples were retained rather than replaced after inspecting their appearance. Recorded inference medians were approximately 80.03–83.18 seconds per case and model, including model loading; combined CPU scoring/evaluation had a median of 19.68 seconds per case. Separate model-load and per-score CPU timings were unavailable, so these measurements do not establish score-specific overhead or speedup. [Sources: E5_fixed_budget, qualitative_model_risks and E5_runtime.](e1_e5_evidence.json)

## Discussion

The principal result is evidence for structural-risk ranking under a specific held-out comparison, together with limits on how that result can be used. E1/E2 quantified foreground and partition contributions and verified the log-count attenuation mechanism. E3 then met the encoded pooled criterion against presence and entropy. However, the stronger partition-only rows prevent a claim that adding the foreground term, or replacing every normalization by joint entropy, universally improves failure detection. The component decomposition is therefore informative even where the combined primary score is not the best-performing row.

Evaluation choices determined which failure-detection question was answered. The case-level normalization effect within annotated scope did not imply an independent benefit from annotation restriction. Predicted outside-scope anatomy, outside-GT foreground, internal matching F1 and missing-volume fraction also described distinct quantities. Reporting them separately exposed conditions that would be obscured by a single uncertainty score or aggregate segmentation endpoint. The observed binary enrichment and weak severe-error enrichment further show that detecting any structural error and prioritizing the most severe errors are different tasks.

The present evidence does not establish practical review savings. AURC summarizes the coverage curve, whereas review utility depends on the operating budget, the failure endpoint and the unit of cost. At the evaluated high-coverage budgets, paired uncertainty did not support a clear merge+split advantage over presence; omission-sensitive results could favor the simpler baseline. Moreover, review was simulated as exclusion of high-score bones, without measuring reader effort or correction quality. The complete-miss example also shows that agreement among members cannot by itself certify correctness. A future omission check would require a separate design and validation study; it is not an improvement evaluated in these experiments.

The scope remains limited to one dataset, three ensemble members and one backend family. Pelvic and femur scans contributed different numbers of bone units, and femur-only primary intervals were less conclusive than pooled intervals. Evaluation used an internal IoU-based contract; its matching-based F1 allowed many-to-one assignments and could conceal partial-volume loss. The supplementary fixed-budget intervals were added after held-out inspection and remain exploratory. Run-bound protocol hashes and correction archives support traceability, but they do not replace an independently verified registration history or an external reproduction. External-dataset performance, human review benefit and new QC policies remain untested.
